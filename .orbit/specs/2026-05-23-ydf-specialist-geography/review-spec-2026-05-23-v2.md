### [HIGH] AC-01's circular-precision guard cue list is not grounded in Sense's source — three of nine cues are wrong

**Category:** assumption
**Pass:** 2
**AC:** ac-01

**Description.** The spec's cue list — `email`, `e-mail`, `mail`, `phone`, `telephone`, `mobile`, `id`, `uuid`, `username` — was authored from intuition about what Sense's "trusted header cues" probably are. Checking against `crates/finetype-model/src/column.rs::header_hint()` (the canonical Sense header-cue function, lines 3844–4150):

- `email`, `e mail`, `email address`, `emailaddress` — present (exact match → `identity.person.email`).
- `mail` — **NOT in the exact-match table**, and the substring matcher does not promote bare "mail" either; the spec's claim that Sense trusts `mail` as a header cue is unsupported.
- `phone`, `telephone`, `mobile` — caught by the substring matcher at line 4098 (`h.contains("phone") || h.contains("tel") || h.contains("mobile") || h.contains("fax")`), routed to `identity.person.phone_number`. Present.
- `id` — **explicitly removed** by decision 0034 (`0034-remove-id-increment-header-hint.yaml`). Comment at line 3958: `"id" | "identifier" — REMOVED (decision 0034). ID columns are genuinely ambiguous`. Sense does not trust `id` as a cue at all. Excluding extract rows where header contains "id" because Sense "trusts" it is incoherent — Sense did not consult the header for those columns; its prediction came from values.
- `uuid`, `guid` — present (line 3875).
- `username` — **not a Sense cue**. The closest is `user name` (with space), which Sense maps to `identity.person.full_name` via the qualified-name path at line 4135, not to a username type.

The deeper issue: the guard's logic is "if Sense's prediction was driven by the header, exclude". That logic depends on knowing *which* of Sense's predictions header-derived. A cue list authored from guesses bakes in wrong assumptions on both sides — it excludes rows that should be kept (`id`-named geography columns where Sense was wrong from values, not the header) and lets through rows that should be excluded (any cue not on the list, e.g. `postcode`, `zip code`, `address`, `street`, `state`, `country` — all of which *are* trusted Sense cues per `column.rs` lines 3933–3951 and would themselves cause symmetric circularity in the other direction).

**Evidence.**
- `/Users/hugh/github/meridian-online/finetype/crates/finetype-model/src/column.rs:3844–4150` — canonical `header_hint()` function.
- `/Users/hugh/github/meridian-online/finetype/.orbit/choices/0034-remove-id-increment-header-hint.yaml` — id cue removed.
- The list of geography-trusted Sense cues that *would* cause the opposite-direction problem (Sense predicts geography from header, YDF predicts non-geography): `latitude`, `lat`, `longitude`, `lng`, `country`, `country code`, `city`, `state`, `province`. None of these are excluded by the current guard — but the spec's row filter only includes rows where Sense was non-geography, so the directional asymmetry is partially intentional. Still: a real cue list grounded in source is the only way to know which direction matters.

**Recommendation.** Amend ac-01 to ground the cue list in `column.rs::header_hint()` rather than assert it inline. One concrete shape:

> The cue list is derived from `crates/finetype-model/src/column.rs::header_hint()` exact-match arms plus the substring matcher at line 4098, restricted to cues that map to non-geography types. The cue list is committed alongside the script as `scripts/header_cues_for_ydf_extract.tsv` (header_cue, sense_type, source_line) and regenerated by an audit script that greps `column.rs` at extract-time. The script errors at startup if its embedded cue list drifts from the audit.

This makes the guard load-bearing on Sense's actual behaviour rather than on a fixed list that ages out with every Sense change.

### [HIGH] AC-03's blocking edge — extract is consumed by v20 — is not separately closable from the observation

**Category:** test-gap
**Pass:** 3
**AC:** ac-03

**Description.** ac-03's `ac_type: observation` is correctly classified for the lift measurement (post-retrain empirical window — METHOD.md band rules legitimately defer it). But the AC bundles two qualitatively different facts:

1. **Code/config: v20's training pipeline consumes `geography.tsv`.** Whether `prepare_multibranch_data.py` (or its v20 successor) reads from `eval/gittables/v20_training_candidates/geography.tsv` is a static fact about training config — verifiable by `grep` the moment v20 trains, without waiting for the corpus pass. If the extract is never wired in, the lift can't materialise; this risk is invisible until the deferred observation completes.
2. **Observation: the lift hits the 20% threshold in both cells.** This is the right shape for `observation` — wait for the corpus pass, read the delta table.

Bundling them under one observation AC means a v20 retrain that silently skipped the geography extract (e.g. the data-prep script's path was never updated) reads as "AC-03 waiting on v20" right up until the post-retrain corpus pass runs and the delta is zero. The blocking failure is detectable *months earlier* if it's its own AC.

**Evidence.**
- `scripts/prepare_multibranch_data.py:2547` shows the existing leakage filter consumes `eval/row_hashes.tsv`; the gittables extract has no path declared anywhere in the script as of this review. Wiring is required code-side; the spec doesn't say where it lands.
- METHOD.md band rules: `code` / `config` block `spec.close`; `observation` defers. A bundled AC inherits the looser band, hiding the blocking fact behind the deferred one.

**Recommendation.** Split ac-03 into two:

- **ac-03a (code/config):** `prepare_multibranch_data.py` (or its v20-promoted equivalent) reads `eval/gittables/v20_training_candidates/geography.tsv` as a training source. Close evidence: a referenced commit + a `grep -n "geography.tsv" scripts/` output showing the path is wired. `ac_type: code` — blocks `spec.close`.
- **ac-03b (observation):** post-v20 corpus pass shows ≥ 20% reduction in both target cells per the existing wording. `ac_type: observation` — defers.

This keeps the deferred half honest about waiting on v20, while making the blocking half visible to `spec.close` and `/orb:review-pr` immediately.

### [MEDIUM] AC-02's verification command depends on prepare_multibranch_data.py exposing a leakage-only entry point

**Category:** test-gap
**Pass:** 2
**AC:** ac-02

**Description.** ac-02 specifies `python3 scripts/prepare_multibranch_data.py --dry-run` as the close-evidence command. `--dry-run` is a real flag (line 2607 of that script), but inspecting the surrounding code: `--dry-run` skips feature extraction (line 47 docstring: "Show counts without extracting features"), not the leakage filter itself. The leakage filter runs at line ~2940 regardless of dry-run state, after distilled/synthetic loading.

That means `--dry-run` is *probably* a valid command to use here, but the spec doesn't actually verify it. Two concrete risks:

1. **`prepare_multibranch_data.py --dry-run` does not currently accept `geography.tsv` as input** — the script loads from `--distilled` (a parquet/csv.gz path) and synthetic generation, not from arbitrary TSV. Pointing it at the geography extract requires either a new `--gittables-extract` flag or rewriting the extract into the existing distilled/synthetic shape. The spec is silent on which.
2. **The verification command emits `row_hash_overlap: N` to stdout**, not a JSON artefact at `eval/gittables/v20_training_candidates/geography.leakage.json`. Producing the JSON requires either capturing/parsing stdout or adding JSON output to the script. The spec asserts the JSON exists as close evidence but doesn't say which spec is responsible for emitting it.

The spec hedges with "or the existing equivalent leakage check" — but that hedge is doing a lot of work. The leakage firewall the spec describes is real and consistent with MADR 0056; the verification mechanism between it and the existing `prepare_multibranch_data.py` is not.

**Evidence.**
- `scripts/prepare_multibranch_data.py:2546–2547` — leakage filter wired to `eval/row_hashes.tsv` (loaded line 2961).
- `scripts/prepare_multibranch_data.py:2552–2557` — input is `--distilled` (a single distilled-data path), not multiple training-data sources.
- `scripts/eval_leakage/__init__.py:72` — `row_hash(header, value)` is the canonical hasher; the geography extract can call it directly to emit row hashes inline, avoiding the prepare_multibranch_data round-trip.

**Recommendation.** Choose one of two paths and write the AC against the one chosen:

- **Path A (preferred): leakage check lives inside the extractor.** The extractor imports `scripts/eval_leakage/__init__.py`'s `row_hash`, computes the hash for each output row, joins against `eval/row_hashes.tsv` itself, errors if any overlap. Emits `geography.leakage.json` directly. No dependency on `prepare_multibranch_data.py`. ac-02 close evidence: the JSON file. This is verifiable purely inside this spec.
- **Path B: extend `prepare_multibranch_data.py` to accept the gittables extract.** This is a larger change to the v19 training pipeline and probably wants its own spec; mention it as a follow-up rather than fold it into ac-02.

Path A is the small move that closes ac-02 cleanly. Path B is what eventually has to happen for ac-03a (training pipeline actually consumes the extract) — but that's the *training pipeline's* job, not the leakage firewall's.

### [MEDIUM] AC-04 conflates the script and the per-domain config block

**Category:** missing-requirement
**Pass:** 1
**AC:** ac-04

**Description.** ac-04 reads: "git diff shows zero scripts/ changes" and parenthetically "a per-domain config block (e.g. domain-specific header cues for the circular-precision guard, declared in a per-domain dict at the top of the script)". These two assertions are in tension:

- If the per-domain config block is a Python dict at the top of `scripts/extract_ydf_specialist_training_data.py`, then adding a `finance` config block IS a change to `scripts/extract_ydf_specialist_training_data.py` — `git diff scripts/` is nonzero, AC fails by its own criterion.
- If the per-domain config block lives in a separate file (e.g. `eval/gittables/ydf_extract_domain_configs.yaml`), then `git diff scripts/` is zero, AC passes — but the test is now under-specified because `scripts/` is the wrong scope. A reviewer can't tell whether the config file is supposed to exist, what its shape is, or where it lives.

The spec is ambiguous about which artefact the close evidence comes from.

**Evidence.** ac-04 description, lines 117–119: "plus a per-domain config block (e.g. domain-specific header cues for the circular-precision guard, declared in a per-domain dict at the top of the script)". The parenthetical "at the top of the script" places the config inside `scripts/extract_ydf_specialist_training_data.py`, which contradicts the "zero scripts/ changes" close evidence.

**Recommendation.** Pick one and rewrite:

- **Option A:** Config dict lives at the top of the extractor script. Close evidence becomes "git diff between geography and finance runs shows changes only inside the `DOMAIN_CONFIGS = { ... }` block, not to any function body. A code-review-style diff inspection is the close evidence, not the empty-diff assertion".
- **Option B (preferred — matches Finding 1's recommendation):** Per-domain configs live in `eval/gittables/ydf_extract_domain_configs.yaml`. Close evidence: `git diff scripts/extract_ydf_specialist_training_data.py` shows zero changes between the geography and finance runs. The YAML is touched, the script isn't.

Option B couples cleanly with Finding 1's recommendation to derive the cue list from `column.rs` at audit time — the audit emits per-domain YAML rather than a single TSV.

### [LOW] AC-01's `sense_prediction_missed` column name is misleading given Finding 1

**Category:** assumption
**Pass:** 1
**AC:** ac-01

**Description.** The output schema names a column `sense_prediction_missed`, framing every row as a confirmed Sense error. After Finding 1's reframe, the more accurate name is something like `sense_prediction_non_geography` or `sense_prediction_for_disagreement` — the column records Sense's prediction in the disagreement set, but whether Sense "missed" depends on adjudication that this extract does not perform. The mislabelled-email columns from the cycle-1 review are the standing demonstration: Sense did not miss those, the dataset author did.

**Evidence.** ac-01 output row tuple (line 15): `sense_prediction_missed`. The cycle-1 review's evidence section (line 46): "every header is an `email` field, every value is a faker-style US address" — Sense was right; the source mislabelled the column.

**Recommendation.** Rename to `sense_prediction` (neutral) and let the schema document say "this is Sense's prediction for the column where YDF disagreed; whether Sense or YDF is correct is the *purpose* of the extract, not its premise". Cheap rename, removes a load-bearing claim from the column name.

## Honest Assessment

The spec is materially stronger than cycle-1's. Three of the four cycle-1 HIGHs are now substantively addressed: leakage firewall has three concrete pieces (Finding 2 is about *how* to verify it, not whether), the 20% threshold pins what cycle-1 left vague, and ac-01's circular-precision guard exists in writing.

The remaining material risk is **Finding 1 — cue list authored from intuition, not source**. The guard is the most surgical of the cycle-1 fixes and the one with the highest cost-of-being-wrong: a guard authored from guesses produces a corpus that still has the email-mislabelling problem (Sense doesn't trust `id` so it doesn't fire; Sense's `username` matching is qualified-only so it doesn't fire; `mail` isn't a cue at all, so headers reading literally `email` only fire on the bare exact match). The fix is small — grep `column.rs` once, commit the cue list as an audited artefact — but it's load-bearing.

**Findings 2 (ac-03 split) and 3 (ac-02 path A vs B)** are about whether close evidence is grounded in code that exists. Both are mechanical rewrites; both materially affect whether the spec ships cleanly or stalls on infrastructure assumptions. The split in Finding 2 in particular makes the difference between "v20 silently never read the extract, AC-03 hangs for months" and "v20 wired the path on day one, observation defers cleanly to the corpus pass".

**Finding 4 (ac-04 ambiguity)** is small but blocking — without picking config-in-script vs config-out-of-script, ac-04 cannot be tested as written.

**Finding 5 (column rename)** is cosmetic; flag only.

Address Findings 1, 2, 3, and 4. Finding 5 is take-or-leave.
